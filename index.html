GLP-1 Drugs and NAION

Does semaglutide or tirzepatide cause optic nerve stroke (non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy, NAION)? This page sets out every published estimate: cohort studies, randomised trials and meta-analyses, with the absolute rates where reported. Every ratio compares people on a GLP-1 drug with people on another treatment; above 1 means more NAION on the GLP-1 drug. Every interval is a 95% confidence interval. Numbers in brackets point to the numbered sources at the foot of the page.

Non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy

Sudden painless loss of vision from poor blood flow to the front of the optic nerve. The concern began with a 2024 Boston cohort and has since been tested in national registers in Scandinavia, the United Kingdom and the United States.1

Clinical takeaways

  1. NAION is probably a real but rare effect in type 2 diabetes. Most large cohort studies and every pooled observational analysis point the same way (relative risk about 1.4 to 2.8), and the European Medicines Agency lists NAION as a very rare side effect of semaglutide, affecting up to 1 in 10,000 users.3,2,4
  2. The absolute excess is small. About 8 extra cases per 100,000 person-years in the pooled diabetes data, or a number needed to harm of about 2,700 to 3,300 over 18 months to 2 years.2,5,6
  3. The risk window is from about 6 months to 2 years of treatment, not the first weeks, with no clear excess after that, and studies weighted by design quality show smaller effects (the US Veterans study with chart-checked codes found 1.35).7,8
  4. Evidence in obesity without diabetes is thin and inconsistent; the large early relative risk (7.6) came from a small referral cohort and has not been reproduced.1,9
  5. The main susceptibility is a small, crowded optic disc (cup-to-disc ratio 0.2 or less), but no study has yet measured whether GLP-1 drugs add more risk in such discs. Stopping or withholding the drug is not recommended on current evidence; a new NAION while on treatment warrants discussion with the prescriber.10,11

Who seems more susceptible

  • A small, crowded optic disc: cup-to-disc ratio 0.2 or less carried 46 times the odds of NAION in 2,169 cases; disc shape was the same whether or not people were on a GLP-1 drug.10
  • Men, people under 50, smokers, and people whose HbA1c fell by 1% or more (UK primary care).15
  • Men and people over 50 in US insurance data.6
  • Semaglutide more than liraglutide; the signal for tirzepatide is weaker, and one study found less NAION on tirzepatide than on other GLP-1 drugs.39,42,30
  • Possible mechanism: a mouse and small human study found that GLP-1 drugs lower intracranial pressure and slow retinal blood flow, reversibly.43 Case reports also describe papillitis and paracentral acute middle maculopathy.44

Why the studies disagree

  • The comparator matters. SGLT2 inhibitors may themselves protect the optic nerve, which would enlarge the apparent GLP-1 effect.22,19
  • Diagnosis codes for NAION are unreliable. Chart review confirmed 74.5% and 84.7% of coded cases in two Veterans studies.14,8
  • Studies from Denmark and Norway report larger effects than those from the global record network, possibly from method rather than population.13,28
  • Randomised trials have too few events: 8 against 4 cases across 69 trials.32
  • Rapid improvement in blood sugar is itself a suspected trigger, as with diabetic retinopathy.15

Sources searched, in order

  • Dr Sheck's journal reading notes (the GLP-1 and the eye page, 14 sources), then the Zotero library ("GLP-1" and "Research Review" collections). Items held there are marked Library in the list of sources.
  • PubMed, searched on 28 October 2026: 22 queries covering NAION and optic neuropathy, glaucoma and eye pressure, AMD, diabetic retinopathy and macular oedema, vascular occlusion, uveitis, cataract, dry eye, intracranial hypertension, trials, meta-analyses and pharmacovigilance, for all GLP-1 receptor agonists and tirzepatide. 300 records were fetched, 151 abstracts were read, and full text was opened for research letters without abstracts.
  • Not found: full text of several research letters (Fung 2025, Goldenberg 2025, Abboud 2026). No study has measured NAION risk by optic disc shape on GLP-1 drugs.

How figures are shown

  • Ratios are shown as published (hazard, risk, odds or rate ratios), oriented so that above 1 means more events on the GLP-1 drug.
  • No new pooling was done: the pooled rows are the published meta-analyses. Many overlap, as they draw on the same cohorts.

Gaps noted

  • No cohort has been stratified by disc at risk, and there is no validated NAION diagnosis code.
  • Suggested New Zealand projects: a disc OCT case-control study, validation of coded NAION, and an interrupted time series of NAION incidence by ethnicity.
